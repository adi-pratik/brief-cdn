## Tonight’s concept (2–3 min)

What moved for us is the mechanism. Thin tape = we use the time to sharpen edge: how to systematically convert cross‑venue basis (our book vs offshore AMMs vs other CLOBs) into predictable market‑maker earnings — without on‑chain rebates and while respecting our fixed 240 bps quadratic taker fee and maker‑0 policy.

- Constraint check
  - PredictStreet fee rails: taker 240 bps quadratic in P(1–P); maker always 0 on‑chain; all MM economics off‑chain (rebates/credits). That means we cannot “buy” flow the AMM way. Our edge must be microstructure (timing, routing) and cross‑venue inventory management, not fees. (Compare: Polymarket charges takers and pays maker rebates programmatically on some books.) ([docs.polymarket.com](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-makers/trading?utm_source=openai))

- The opportunity: persistent basis
  - Cross‑venue price gaps on the same condition persist for minutes in thin hours because of fee asymmetry, differing void/settlement rules, and retail latency. Basis = predictable vig if you can lift/take on one venue and make on the other within net fees and slippage.

- Rails that let us actually capture it
  - Brazil PIX and India UPI materially cut funding latency for retail and MMs. Faster fiat↔USDC top‑ups shrink the time between spotting a gap and inventory getting paid. PIX policy keeps tightening but remains instant rails; UPI keeps adding operational guardrails and features for scale. Use both in our onboarding and MM funding playbooks. ([bcb.gov.br](https://www.bcb.gov.br/estabilidadefinanceira/exibenormativo?numero=746&tipo=Instru%C3%A7%C3%A3o+Normativa+BCB&utm_source=openai))

- How to run the play (inside our constraints)
  1) Price map: maintain a live cross‑venue fair for each mapped slug, tagging each with fee‑adjusted executable mid and depth. Penalize venues whose rules/voids diverge.
  2) Routing: when |basis| > threshold after fees and projected slippage, route taker to the cheaper leg and incentivize maker inventory on our book with off‑chain credits that vest on net-tightening quotes and time‑at‑touch, not raw volume (rebate‑farming resistant).
  3) Inventory: target neutral overnight; during event hours, allow small directional skew only when the oracle cadence or resolution window favors us (adverse selection filter). 
  4) Latency budget: co‑locate price listeners where counterpart venues publish books; avoid self‑match/wash traps by excluding same master from rebateable fills.

- Why now
  - Offshore competitors are actively re‑tooling rails and incentives (e.g., SX gasless path; legacy token sunset). Expect their fee/latency profiles to shift and create fresh, short‑lived bases we can farm with disciplined routing. ([blog.sx.bet](https://blog.sx.bet/news/sunsetting-the-sx-token-and-new-infrastructure-plans/?utm_source=openai))

- Compliance guardrails to keep the edge sustainable
  - Map each venue’s RG/investor‑protection perimeter before listing or routing. UK/GBR and EU regulators are tightening risk/FRAs/MiCA record‑keeping; UAE ADGM keeps publishing AML/cyber notices. Our ops must inherit the heavier of the two standards when politics/economics/sports overlap. ([gamblingcommission.gov.uk](https://www.gamblingcommission.gov.uk/news/article/commission-to-introduce-financial-risk-assessments-in-staged-approach?utm_source=openai))

Bottom line: We can’t change maker fees. We can change how quickly we see, fund, and clear the spread — and pay makers off‑chain for the inventory that actually narrows it.
