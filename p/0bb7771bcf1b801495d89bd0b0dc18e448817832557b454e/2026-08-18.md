## The one thing
If the vig feels heavy, retail won’t lean into the book; if the tape feels fair, they spray the ladder. We can’t change our on‑chain taker fee (240 bps, quadratic), so we have to change how it feels: depth at the touch, predictable fills, and a reason for makers to warehouse inventory.

## Mechanism — make 240 bps feel like 80 bps
- Maker ladders at the touch: pre‑seed 1–3 ticks on both sides with minimum displayed sizes and replenish rules. That narrows the displayed spread and reduces slippage. On a CLOB, the spread is a personality test — tonight we make it boring. (We run a CLOB; not an AMM.)
- Time‑in‑force that matches intent: add fill‑or‑kill (FOK) and immediate‑or‑cancel (IOC) presets to the quick‑trade UI so takers stop paying for partials they didn’t want. Predictable execution beats tiny price improvements.
- Off‑chain MM accruals: since maker on‑chain is 0 forever, tilt rebates off‑chain to pay for top‑of‑book presence and quote persistence under stress; penalize fade/ghost quotes. That’s our lever.
- Short‑horizon sports: restrict tick sizes and quote bands tighter for 5m/30m/1h books so the effective spread stays small while the oracle cadence protects us from adverse selection. Define resolution sources crisply to avoid void tax. 
- Best‑ex and fair display: our rulebook should mirror “best execution” spirit from mature regimes (disclose routing, slippage stats, and venue outages). If the book is fair and up, users forgive the vig. ([en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com](https://en.adgm.thomsonreuters.com/entiresection/19378?utm_source=openai))

## Why this maps to our stack
- ADI CLOB + USDC vaults → instant credit, so we can enforce FOK/IOC without user confusion.
- 0 maker fee on‑chain + off‑chain rebates → pay for inventory where it matters: at the touch, during TV timeouts, and at injury news.
- Resolution discipline → fewer voids, more trust; we reference licensed data and keep a challenge window.
