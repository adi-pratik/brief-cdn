18 Aug — Concept issue: Maker rebates, quadratic vig, and a sports-first play for LATAM/MENA

Why now
- The last 24h had no material regulator or venue moves we can act on. We’re spending the commute on a liquidity design we can ship without waiting for anyone.

The constraint (ours)
- PredictStreet is a CLOB. Taker fee is a fixed 240 bps, quadratic in P*(1–P). Maker fee on-chain is always 0. MM economics live off‑chain.
- Translation to quotes: widest taker friction sits where P≈0.5. If we don’t compensate makers there, they either pull size or post decorative quotes and let the basis drift.

Guardrails by geo (what we must model into copy and rails)
- Brazil: SPA’s operator guidance says authorized fixed‑odds operators can’t offer unregulated games (e.g., P2P/fantasy/skill) — prediction‑style P2P UX needs a true fixed‑odds wrapper to sit inside Brazil’s regime. That shapes what front‑ends we build or partner with in‑country. ([gov.br](https://www.gov.br/fazenda/pt-br/composicao/orgaos/secretaria-de-premios-e-apostas/apostas-de-quota-fixa/questoes-tecnicas/?utm_source=openai))
- UAE/ADGM: FSRA’s FCCP notices keep hammering VASP AML hygiene, including exposure to unlicensed VA activity — practical reading is pre‑trade and onboarding checks on counterparties/payments, and crisp logs on who our MMs are. Design your MM program like it’s audit‑ready. ([adgm.com](https://www.adgm.com/operating-in-adgm/financial-and-cyber-crime-prevention/notices-and-circulars?utm_source=openai))
- New Zealand: DIA’s online‑casino licensing is running via an ascending‑clock auction (EOI closed Aug 14; auction in Sept). Not our core vertical, but useful: their choice of auction format is exactly the style of transparent price discovery we can mirror for allocating off‑chain MM rebate budgets. ([dia.govt.nz](https://www.dia.govt.nz/Online-Gambling-for-Providers?utm_source=openai))

The playbook (actions we can take without code debt)
1) Off‑chain MM rebates that pay for width where our vig bites most.
   - Tier A markets (P in 40–60% bands): pay per posted and maintained depth at 1–2 ticks inside a target spread ladder; pay more for kept quotes through volatility. Settlement weekly in USDC; clawbacks for last‑look/quote flicker.
   - Tier B markets (tails): smaller per‑unit depth bounties; goal is presence, not perfect micro‑structure.
2) Sports first, cricket + football.
   - List dense slates with uniform rules (voids, ET/penalties) and one data oracle — lowers adverse‑selection and lets makers recycle inventory across correlated markets.
   - Build combo books later; start with mutually exclusive 1X2 and simple props that settle cleanly from licensed feeds.
3) Rails that convert: PIX in BR, UPI in IN.
   - Treat BRLPIX→USDC and INRUPI→USDC legs as product, not back‑office. Circle’s prior integrations in Brazil point to an activation story we can copy: deposit predictably in minutes, not days. UX copy should say so. ([circle.com](https://www.circle.com/blog/usdc-now-available-in-brazil-and-mexico-through-national-payment-systems-with-local-currency?utm_source=openai))
4) Risk knobs the desk owns (no code freeze required):
   - Quote‑time SLAs (e.g., 95% uptime during listed windows), post‑only recovery windows after engine restarts, and per‑market inventory caps by maker.
   - A single, public “spread ladder” per sport that MMs can trade against. Publish it; pay on it. The house (us) doesn’t make markets — we pay for them.

Teach-in: why this should work
- Makers hate unknowns: give them fixed bounties for being top‑of‑book at defined sizes, and they’ll bring capital. Our 0 maker fee means all maker economics must be off‑chain; that’s fine if the schedule is transparent and audited.
- The quadratic taker means the book’s mid gets sticky at 50/50. Paying for depth there makes the whole book feel tighter, even for retail flow hitting 35/65 markets.
