So we spend the slot on a differentiator we can ship: combo books (parlays) on a CLOB.

What to build next

- Problem to solve: parlay appetite is real (sports users expect it), but naïve combo UX on a CLOB magnifies vig, leaks edge to arbers, and can starve single‑legs of depth.
- Constraints we must respect:
  - Our fee constraint: 240 bps quadratic taker; maker 0 on-chain; all MM economics are off-chain.
  - Oracle/voids: leg‑by‑leg void rules cause non‑intuitive payouts if not specified at order time.
  - Resolution: we resolve from licensed feeds with a challenge window; no ad‑hoc operator calls.
- Competitive context:
  - Azuro just pushed predefined combos in-tooling (one‑tap “express” picks) for apps building on their AMM rails. That’s an AMM story, not a CLOB, but it sets user expectation. 

Design we can ship (V1)

- Quote-as-basket RFQ: user picks 2–6 legs; we produce a single “basket” quote off-chain, pre‑priced by our MMs against current book depth; fill posts to chain as N linked child orders + 1 basket receipt.
- Price integrity guardrails:
  - Basis cap: enforce per‑leg slippage caps vs displayed top-of-book at RFQ time; if crossed at commit, auto‑reprice or cancel.
  - Dependency checks: forbid mutually exclusive selections that would overstate the payout (“same-game traps”).
  - Voids: publish leg-void ladder ex‑ante (void leg → price-to-remaining; postponed → keep; abandoned → basket void). Users see it before commit.
- Economics:
  - Keep taker fee on the basket not on each leg. Internally pro‑rate to legs for P&L and MM rebates. Target effective vig ≤ sportsbook parlays at 3–4 legs.
- MM tooling:
  - Basket quote API with “combinator” risk haircut and per‑leg hedge suggestions. Show delta to existing inventory to avoid becoming the house.

Why it matters

- We add a sports‑native UX (combos) while staying a P2P intermediary. The basket RFQ keeps price formation on our CLOB, instead of routing to a separate parlay AMM with different risk.
