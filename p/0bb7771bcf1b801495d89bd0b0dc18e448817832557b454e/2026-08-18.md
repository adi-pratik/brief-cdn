The mechanism (why prices diverge across venues even on the same question)
- Quadratic taker fees: Many CLOBs (including ours) charge a fee proportional to p·(1−p). Polymarket publishes the exact curve and category multipliers; makers pay 0, takers pay a fee that peaks at 50%. That curve alone shifts the economically fair mid vs. a no‑fee book. ([docs.polymarket.com](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees?utm_source=openai))
- Maker rebates: On Polymarket, 20–25% of taker fees are rebated to makers by category. A quote that looks “too tight” may be rational if the maker is effectively earning a fee stream. ([docs.polymarket.com](https://docs.polymarket.com/market-makers/maker-rebates?utm_source=openai))
- AMM vs CLOB inventory: AMM pools (e.g., Thales) socialize inventory and price impact differently than a CLOB. Pool caps, utilization, and LP PnL targets mean the AMM fair can sit off the CLOB mid and stay there. ([docs.thalesmarket.io](https://docs.thalesmarket.io/using-thales/providing-liquidity-for-thales-amm?utm_source=openai))

So what: Basis isn’t just “inefficiency.” It’s fee‑and‑inventory risk in the tape. For PredictStreet, with a fixed 240 bps quadratic taker and maker 0 on‑chain, the natural wedge vs. venues with different fee curves and rebate funding will persist unless a single participant internalizes both sides and the rebate math.
