Title: 18 Aug — The spread is a personality test

What changed for us
- Nothing material in-window. So we use the slot to sharpen the playbook that decides whether our book is tight and deep or wide and empty.

Concept — How makers win on PredictStreet (and why takers follow)
- Our constraints: taker fee is a fixed 240 bps, quadratic in P*(1–P); maker on-chain is always 0; any MM economics are off-chain rebates. That means price improves fill probability mainly via tighter quotes, not fee discounts.
- Maker priorities:
  1) Width: Quote to the fee-adjusted mid. The taker’s effective cost is raw spread + fee; your job is to make that combined price acceptable. Rule of thumb: at 50–50, takers eat 240 bps; quote a 60–80 bps raw spread and you’re live.
  2) Size and laddering: Post meaningful size at the top two price levels and a gentle ladder behind it. Aim for depth where takers actually cross — not theatrical size 6 ticks away.
  3) Time-in-force: Use GTC for the backbone, IOC/PO (post-only) for surgical top-of-book refreshes. You want queue priority, not churn.
  4) Inventory bands: Pre-commit inventory caps per side per market; expand/contract with realized vol. Your edge dies when you chase fills into a one-way tape.
  5) Cross-venue basis: If Polymarket or others run rich/cheap versus us, check rules/fees first; only then lean into the basis with capped size. Basis ≠ free lunch; it’s often a rules mismatch.
- Taker UX implications: If our raw spread + fee is competitive, the taker sees slippage they can price. If not, they see a tax and bounce. We win by making the combined tax small, predictable, and stable.

Playbook we’d ship
- Maker tools: one-click ladder templates (50–50, skewed, wide), queue-position hints, and an inventory band dashboard with auto-halts.
- Off-chain rebates: volume-tiered with anti-wash controls; pay for displayed top-of-book time and executed size, not ping-pong.
- Monitoring: alert on fee-adjusted spread and top-two-level depth per flagship market; page an MM lead if either metric degrades for 10+ minutes near kick/print.

Why this matters
- CLOB vs AMM: An AMM socializes inventory; we don’t. Tight books require intentional inventory management and rebates that reward displayed liquidity — within our fee constraint. That’s our moat.
