## Deep cut
ForecastEx binaries are fully collateralised. Customer cash sits in a Clearinghouse Collateral Account and can be invested under Rule 612. Residue after fees was a monthly coupon that FCMs *had* to pass to customers. That second hop is now optional.

The coupon is carry on notional while you are wrong: both sides of a paired book can earn it. That is the product ForecastEx sold — interest even if the prediction fails. IBKR (affiliate / majority owner via IBG) still advertises it as of this afternoon: interest-like incentive coupon on closing market value, daily accrual, monthly pay, **3.13% APY**, rates subject to change. Robinhood Derivatives is a clearing member.

40.6 did not kill the coupon. It killed the customer’s rulebook right. Waterfall is now: DCO → Member; Member may keep it. On a penny-a-side fully-collateralised book, 3% carry on open interest is the real economic story. After today an FCM can treat it as rebate, loyalty lever, or P&L. IBKR can keep paying because it chooses to. Robinhood can match, shade, or pocket. Divergence, if it happens, will show up in retail flow between those two apps.

This is not a fee schedule. ForecastEx pairing is still a penny a side. Our taker is a fixed **240 bps**, quadratic in `P*(1-P)`, immutable for the life of the market. On-chain maker fee is always **0**. MM economics are off-chain accruals only. A “match ForecastEx carry” conversation is yield on idle USDC in ADI vaults — treasury + product — not a maker/taker CR and not a DCM coupon we can clone. We are a Gibraltar betting intermediary. We do not have FCM members or CFTC-style investment of customer segregated funds.

## Competitive
Do not collapse wrappers. Kalshi / Polymarket US = CFTC DCMs, event contracts as swaps. ForecastEx = DCM + DCO, IBG-controlled, distributed via IBKR and Robinhood. PredictStreet = GLA 167 betting intermediary, off-chain CLOB, USDC, Conditional Tokens. A sportsbook is the house.

If IBKR keeps the 3.13% line and Robinhood does not, carry becomes a distributor feature, not a venue feature. If both stop, ForecastEx loses “paid even when wrong” and becomes a plain binary. That is a US-retail comparison change, not a schedule we ship.

JPMorgan dropping Polymarket’s operating account while (per FT) still courting IPO fees is a bank risk posture. Our rails are USDC/ADI. Any fiat-rail or B2B bank conversation inherits that posture. Press, not a legal opinion.
