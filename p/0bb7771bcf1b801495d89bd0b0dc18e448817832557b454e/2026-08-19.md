Concept — The vig is a personality test: how fee design shows up in your book

- What traders feel
  - Takers feel the fee in the first fill and in every partial; makers feel it in adverse selection. Tight spreads die if taker fee overwhelms edge at the inside.
- Three common fee shapes you’ll meet
  1) Stake-based rake (casino-style): charge on staked notional regardless of P&L — suppresses turnover at fair odds, rewards long-shot punts.
  2) Profit-only rake (exchange-style): charge a % of positive P&L — headline looks friendly; hidden cost appears in skewed resting liquidity if makers can’t recover inventory drift.
  3) Probability-shaped taker (our constraint): fixed 240 bps quadratic in P·(1–P); maker 0 on-chain; MM economics via off-chain rebates.
- Why the same headline ‘1%’ trades very differently across venues
  - An AMM that charges 1% of “profit” but internalizes flow is still the house; a CLOB charging 1% of profit to takers pushes makers to widen to fund expected losses. Users see it as slippage, not a fee line item.
- What this means for PredictStreet
  - Keep maker 0 on-chain. Push rebates off-chain, targeted by time-at-touch and queue contribution.
  - Build a visible “effective fee” widget that converts fee + expected slippage at the top of book into one number before the user taps Buy.
