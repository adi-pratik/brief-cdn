19 Aug — Concept issue (null night)

What moved (concept, not news)
- The most important microstructure lever in short‑horizon markets is the resolution source. A single‑tick snapshot ("what was BTC/USD at 10:05:00") concentrates edge into milliseconds; a TWAP window (30–60s) spreads it across time, shifting P&L from latency/arb bots toward inventory that can carry. That’s not theory; it’s exactly how exchanges hedge settlement risk on expiring perps and how some venues now resolve 5m/15m crypto books via Chainlink Data Streams (TWAP) instead of an exchange print. ([chain.link](https://chain.link/data-streams?utm_source=openai))

Why it matters to PredictStreet (inference)
- Our fee math (240 bps taker, quadratic in P*(1–P); maker 0 on‑chain; MM rebates off‑chain) works best when last‑second snipes don’t hoover EV from passive quotes. TWAP resolution dampens that snipe. That widens the viable maker spread for a given risk budget and lowers the compensation we must pay off‑chain to keep queues populated.
- A TWAP also hardens the oracle surface: one bad tick doesn’t void a book; resolution depends on a stream that can be independently mirrored and verified.

What we should do (Act)
1) Standardize: use a 30s TWAP for 5m markets and 60s for 15m/4h where the underlying supports it. Publish the window, the feed ID, and a JSON example in each market’s Rules.
2) Safeguards: document tie‑breaks (“Up on tie”), publish drift tolerances between our displayed price and the oracle stream, and add a visible “resolution clock” in‑UI during the window.
3) Liquidity program: re‑cut MM rebates to pay for time‑at‑touch and queue depth across the whole window, not just last‑look fills.
4) Verification: run a public mirror of the TWAP stream with daily hashes, and backfill downloadable parquet for audit.

Heads‑up for builders
- Chainlink Data Streams + venue RTDS sockets are the right integration pattern. If a few seconds are missing in the public restream, we must define interpolation rules in advance (linear or “drop and renorm”). Don’t invent them after a close.
