Title — The basis is a leak: how we stop paying it

What this is
- A crisp playbook to shrink persistent cross‑venue basis (our price vs a rival’s on the same question) without handing the edge to the vig.

Why we care
- When our mid diverges from a comparable book, traders arb us and the fee plus slippage becomes their PnL. That’s a leak from our liquidity/MM budget, not a growth engine.

Mechanism — the five levers
1) Contract spec parity: basis is often rules drift. Mandate a pre‑list checklist (resolution source hierarchy, voids, settlement clock). If the oracle ladders differ, price must differ — that’s not a leak, it’s risk.
2) Fee math awareness: with a fixed 240 bps quadratic taker and 0 maker on‑chain, every cross‑venue clip must clear that after spreads. Market makers get rebates off‑chain only; model their hurdle explicitly in routing.
3) Spread discipline on open: open wide, size small, and programmatically tighten into flow. Early tight spreads invite adverse selection; late tight spreads keep the tape honest.
4) Quote continuity: no sudden tick jumps. Use a “no‑gaps” ladder so markouts don’t punish makers and widen the book after a hit.
5) Event time granularity: short‑horizon books (5m/30m/1h crypto) behave like options with jump risk. Demand deeper queues at near‑expiry; gate maker rebates to time‑at‑touch, not raw volume, to avoid rebate farming.

Controls and telegraphing
- Publish one public market‑rules diff per mirrored market (why our rules differ from Venue X). Transparency kills fake basis.
- Add a maker PnL panel in the MM portal: net of taker fee impact, markout, and our off‑chain rebate accruals.

Success metric
- Rolling 7‑day cross‑venue basis < 50 bps at the mid for matched rules; maker markout within ±10 bps around zero on high‑volume hours.

Notes
- If the competitor is running maker rebates on a schedule we can’t mirror on‑chain, simulate it off‑chain and cap weekly budget ex‑wash.
