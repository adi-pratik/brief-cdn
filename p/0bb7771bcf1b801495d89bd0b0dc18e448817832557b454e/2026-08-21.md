The play: earning flow (and PnL) without maker rebates

Why now

What matters to PredictStreet
- Our constraints: fully collateralised CLOB, quadratic taker fee at 240 bps × P×(1−P), maker 0 on‑chain; all MM economics are off‑chain accruals.
- Translation: we can’t buy flow with on‑chain rebates. We have to quote smart, fill first, and keep inventory tame when the book gets one‑sided.

How to win the book anyway
1) Price the fee into the spread
- The taker’s effective cost is the fee; at the wings (P≈0 or 1) fee impact shrinks; at the middle (P≈0.5) it’s largest. Quote widths that scale with P×(1−P). If you quote flat ticks, you’re giving away EV at 50% and scaring fills at the tails.

2) Make the clock work for you
- Use post‑only windows around deploys and small, frequent quote refreshes (no sweeping) to avoid getting crossed by latency takers when news hits. In live sports, prefer IOC for defensive clips; in pre‑game, GTC+pegged‑to‑mid replacement logic (off‑chain) keeps you present without churn.

3) Inventory corridors, not targets
- Set corridor limits by venue pair: core book vs the offshore mirror (Polymarket et al.). When basis opens beyond your fee‑adjusted threshold, lean the side that pays to close it. Basis that persists is either rules or fees — treat it as inventory you get paid to hold, not something to panic‑flatten.

4) Quote the outcome, hedge the state
- Sports: hedge correlated legs (match result vs draw‑no‑bet vs totals) with a house curve for live time decay. Non‑sports: hedge via nearest liquid proxy (ETF, index, alt venue) only when slippage < expected adverse selection.

5) Toxic‑flow discipline
- Add micro‑delays only where your oracle cadence or feed latency makes you blind (live points markets). Otherwise keep matching clean; protect with size tiers and auto‑widen rules on sudden imbalance, not blanket holds.

6) Pay for performance off‑chain
- Since on‑chain maker fee = 0, concentrate your MM credits where spreads tighten and depth actually shows up — not on raw volume. Exclude self‑match/wash, decay credits by time‑at‑touch, and bonus improves in high P×(1−P) zones where taker pain is real.

Metrics that matter (weekly)
- Realised edge vs mid at fill (maker), post‑fee edge (taker), inventory VaR by book, time‑at‑touch share, cross‑venue basis capture, and void‑rate (oracle + rules clarity). If edge is green but basis capture is red, you’re quoting fine and routing poorly.

So what
- We can’t bribe flow with rebates; we can earn it with better microstructure. The most reliable edge available to us is quoting widths that breathe with fee geometry, plus ruthless basis capture rules when competitors price differently. That’s product (order types, TIFs, post‑only toggles), not marketing.
