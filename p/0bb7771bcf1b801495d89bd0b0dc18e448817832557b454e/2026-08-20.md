Concept — Buying tight spreads without changing fees

- Our constraint: taker fee fixed at 240 bps, quadratic in P·(1−P); maker on‑chain is 0; MM economics must live off‑chain.
- The market reality: order books tighten where the house pays for it. Competitors use per‑market fee curves and maker rebates to literally buy midpoint quotes. Example references: Polymarket’s category fee curves and maker rebate pool, Thales/Overtime’s AMM “Safebox” backstop for inventory shocks, and AMM‑first stacks (Azuro) routing all bets through pooled risk. ([docs.polymarket.com](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees?utm_source=openai))

What we can do now (no rule changes)

- Time‑boxed rebate ladders: pay more off‑chain where adverse selection is worst (match open, last 15m, injury/VAR windows). Reward quoted size inside X bps of mid for Y seconds; penalize flicker quotes.
- Midpoint targeting: pay on realized inside spread vs. book mid, not just posted size. If the taker lifts you, great; if you fade, no rebate.
- Coverage quotas > vanity size: bonuses for keeping both sides present across the market set (ML, spread, total, 1H) so takers see a complete book.
- Circuit‑breaker inventory aid: small off‑chain “buffer pot” to subsidize quotes when oracle latency or one‑sided flow spikes, inspired by AMM backstops, without warehousing risk ourselves. ([docs.thalesmarket.io](https://docs.thalesmarket.io/thales-parimutuel-markets/safebox?utm_source=openai))

Why it matters

- Tighter inside markets raise fill probability and session length, not just top‑of‑book screenshots. The vig doesn’t have to move if the quotes do.
- This is how we meet sports fans where the action actually is: right before kick, at halftime, and when chaos hits.
