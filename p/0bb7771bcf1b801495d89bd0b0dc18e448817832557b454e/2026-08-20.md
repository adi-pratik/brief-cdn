Concept tonight: The vig is the product

Why this matters
- On a CLOB betting exchange, the house doesn’t set odds — liquidity does. The price you feel is the taker fee and the rebate the maker expects. Get that wrong and spreads widen, depth thins, and basis opens versus rival venues.

Mechanism (fast)
- Maker vs taker: Makers post quotes, carry inventory risk; takers cross the spread and pay the vig. Our production fees: 240 bps taker (quadratic in P*(1−P)), maker 0 on‑chain; MM economics come via off‑chain rebates. That means we can’t “discount” takers per market; we have to design rebates and quoting tools that still net a tight market.
- Adverse selection: Takers hit when they know more; makers need protection (posting tools, post‑only windows, peg‑to‑mid, and time‑at‑price priority). If we help makers manage inventory, they quote more size, and takers still clear at fair total cost.
- Cross‑venue basis: Persistent price gaps vs other books are usually (a) rules/oracle differences, (b) fee math, or (c) thin depth. Diagnose before you subsidise.

What we should do (3 bullets, actionable)
- Ship maker utilities: post‑only + peg‑to‑mid + quote‑size ladders and per‑market inventory caps so makers can tighten without blowing through risk.
- Stand up a basis sentry: continuous compare of our top‑of‑book vs two named venues for mapped markets; auto‑page MMs when basis > fee‑adjusted threshold for N minutes.
- LATAM/APAC rails: ensure buy/sell USDC with PIX/UPI partners is one tap in‑app; reduce friction at the edge so the book fills faster when the line moves.

Vocabulary (for the team)
- Spread: best offer − best bid; personality test for our market’s health.
- Depth: executable size near mid; makers create it, takers consume it.
- Slippage: worse fill vs your desired odds when the book moves.
- Basis: cross‑venue price gap net of rules and fees.
