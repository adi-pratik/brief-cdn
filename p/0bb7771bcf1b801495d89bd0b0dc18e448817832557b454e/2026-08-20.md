Tonight’s concept — The vig is a personality test

- The setup: We run a CLOB with a quadratic taker fee and zero on‑chain maker fee. Off‑chain maker economics are the only knob we’re allowed to turn. Polymarket US publicly documents a similar fee functional form and recently inched its taker coefficient up, which is a clean external reference for how small fee moves change order book behavior near 50%. (See cites.)
- Why this matters: A quadratic fee charges most at p≈0.50 — where adverse selection risk is highest — and least at the wings. If our maker side is 0 on‑chain, the entire "how do I inventory this?" problem gets pushed into our off‑chain rebates and inventory management.
- Basis mechanics: Persistent cross‑venue price gaps (“basis”) between us and a competitor are almost always one of three things: (1) a fee shape mismatch, (2) a rules/oracle mismatch, or (3) thin depth with inventory aversion. Tonight’s takeaway: fee shape is the quiet killer around 45–55%.
- Concrete levers we control now:
  1) Off‑chain MM rebates that scale with time‑at‑touch and posted size near the midpoint (not just filled volume) — reward displayed depth where our fee bites hardest.
  2) Tick/lot settings that make it cheap to lean (wider ticks = fewer price levels = lower microstructure drag). Use bigger ticks on low‑liquidity culture/crypto minutes; smaller on marquee sports finals.
  3) A standing “basis bounty” for market makers who compress cross‑venue gaps on tracked pairs; pay on realized shrinkage vs our ps-onchain-analytics mapping.
- Guardrails: Don’t chase basis that’s really a rules gap. Read the payout notes and void rules first. Then pay for depth, not wash. Our maker fee is 0 on‑chain; any rebate must exclude self‑match and obvious circular flow.
