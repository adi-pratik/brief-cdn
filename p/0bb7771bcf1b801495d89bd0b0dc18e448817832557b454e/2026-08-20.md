What we’re teaching: Running short-horizon, in‑play markets on a CLOB
- Instrument: 5m/30m/1h score/possession/shot-on-target ladders differ from result markets. Inventories decay fast; adverse selection hides in the next 15 seconds, not next week.
- Fee math: Our taker fee is 240 bps quadratic in P*(1−P). Maker is 0 on‑chain; all MM economics are off‑chain rebates. Price anything someone will *hit* through that vig, not through wishful midpoints.
- Spreads and depth: The spread is a personality test. Narrow for states with slower information (throw‑ins, halftime) and widen immediately after high‑impact ticks (goals, VAR checks). Depth tiers and iceberg orders soak meta‑order flow.
- Time‑in‑force (TIF): Post‑only and immediate‑or‑cancel keep makers from getting run over when latency/congestion spikes. Good‑til‑cancel clutters the book on fast tapes; clip it.
- Oracles and voids: Use licensed feeds with a challenge window. Write void rules like you’ll have to read them out loud to angry traders — because you will. Void scope should be pre‑committed and minimally discretionary.
- Cross‑venue basis: Persistent price gaps across venues are almost always rule or fee differences, not alpha. Diagnose before you chase.
- Rails: Pre‑seed liquidity where your deposits settle fastest (USDC rails first). Don’t promise instant settlement on fiat rails that won’t clear at halftime.

Why it matters to PS

- Our constraints (CLOB, taker 240 bps, maker 0 on‑chain) are features if we make them legible in the UI and generous in off‑chain MM deals.
