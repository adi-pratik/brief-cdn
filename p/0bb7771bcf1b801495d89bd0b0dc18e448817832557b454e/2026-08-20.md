Tonight is a concept issue. The regulatory and venue scans in the last 24h didn’t clear the bar, so we’re spending the commute on how to win the book: CLOB vs AMM vs parimutuel, cross‑venue basis, and what we should ship given our 240 bps quadratic taker fee and maker 0 on‑chain.

What matters for PredictStreet
- We are a CLOB betting intermediary. Inventory sits with traders and market makers, not with us. AMMs and parimutuel pools hold inventory and socialise P/L. That changes how vig is collected, how adverse selection bites, and what MM deals actually work. ([docs.thalesmarket.io](https://docs.thalesmarket.io/?utm_source=openai))
- Thales (parimutuel / AMM) prices positions from oracles and carves out a “Safebox” fee (community‑governed; has moved between 1–3%) as a backstop for AMM insolvency risk—this is inventory socialised as protocol policy, not a maker/taker schedule. That’s a different UX and risk promise than our CLOB. ([docs.thalesmarket.io](https://docs.thalesmarket.io/thales-parimutuel-markets/safebox?utm_source=openai))
- Azuro’s vAMM pushes sell‑side odds, with revenue shares across front‑ends, LPs, and data providers (docs show a 70/20/10 split today). Apps monetise via the spread; LPs wear the book. This is not comparable to a maker rebate; it’s a pool P/L split. ([dev-gem.azuro.org](https://dev-gem.azuro.org/knowledge-hub/faqs/contracts?utm_source=openai))
- Pool LPs face “new‑position drag” (likely negative in early days) because they’re passively warehousing outcome risk across thousands of markets. On a CLOB, our makers are selective and time‑in‑force aware; their inventory is optional, not mandated by pool design. ([dev-gem.azuro.org](https://dev-gem.azuro.org/knowledge-hub/how-azuro-works/protocol-actors/liquidity-providers?utm_source=openai))

Terms in one breath
- Spread = best ask minus best bid; it’s a personality test for liquidity risk.
- Depth = size available near mid without moving the price.
- Slippage = price impact of your own order.
- Maker vs taker = add resting liquidity vs lift it; on PS, taker 240 bps (quadratic in P(1–P)), maker 0 on‑chain; all MM economics are off‑chain credits.
- Basis (cross‑venue) = price difference on identical or near‑identical markets; it’s either fees, rules, or liquidity.

Where to lean in (product + rails)
- Maker tools, not rebates: pre‑trade protection (fill‑or‑kill, post‑only, maker‑only listing), plus cross‑venue mirroring and cancel/replace speed to cut adverse selection.
- Short‑horizon “speed books” on a CLOB: Thales runs speed markets (AMM, Pyth). We should prototype micro‑maturities (5–30 min) in sports phases with explicit time‑in‑force and quote protection—our differentiator is limit‑order control and challenge‑window resolution. ([docs.thalesmarket.io](https://docs.thalesmarket.io/thales-parimutuel-markets/speed-markets?utm_source=openai))
- LATAM rails: USDC already supports local PIX/SPEI on‑ and off‑ramps. If we light up PIX first‑class for deposits/withdrawals around matchdays, we steal payment conversion without touching the house model. ([circle.com](https://www.circle.com/blog/usdc-now-available-in-brazil-and-mexico-through-national-payment-systems-with-local-currency?utm_source=openai))

Risk/Trust lens
- AMM “backstops” (like Safebox) express protocol risk policy; our analogue is listing rules, voids, and oracle challenge windows. EU crypto now treats market‑abuse‑style behaviors under MiCA; even if we’re gambling‑framed, our economic/politics/sports books should inherit the heavier disclosure/controls bias. ([esma.europa.eu](https://www.esma.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2025-10/ESMA75-113276571-1625_Compliance_table_Guidelines_on_market_abuse_MiCA.pdf?utm_source=openai))

The play
- Don’t chase rebate wars we cannot win on‑chain. Win on maker ergonomics, cancel/replace speed, and rails where money actually lands (PIX/UPI next, then UAE corridors).
