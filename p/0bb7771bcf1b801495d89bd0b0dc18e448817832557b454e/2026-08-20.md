The mechanism in one page
- Quadratic taker fees move with probability. A common schedule is fee = C × feeRate × p × (1−p). Peak cost is at 50%, lower at the tails. Makers pay zero; taker fees fund maker rebates. Competitors publish this openly. [cited] ([docs.polymarket.com](https://docs.polymarket.com/trading/fees))
- Why we care: our production taker fee is a fixed 240 bps, quadratic in P(1−P), maker 0 on‑chain; all MM economics are off‑chain accruals. We cannot change the on‑chain maker fee market‑by‑market. Our edge lives in how we rebate and how we protect quotes.

What it does to our CLOB
- Spread: The fee function is highest where markets trade most (near 50%). If we don’t rebate or protect quotes, takers widen us for free and makers stand down when inventory bites.
- Depth: Rebate design determines whether depth appears at the touch or two ticks back. Pay it wrong and you buy fake size that vanishes when the book moves.
- Basis: Cross‑venue mispricing persists when fee curves and void rules differ. Before yelling “arb!”, check rules/fees first; sometimes the basis is the business model.

How to win within our constraints (PS specifics)
- Keep maker 0 on‑chain (hard rule) but pay for true resting size off‑chain. Pay on time‑at‑touch and executed maker notional, haircut self‑match. Penalize cancel‑replace spam around whistle/coin‑flip events.
- Post‑only by default for MMs; cancel‑on‑trigger for stale quotes; and a tiny step‑away timer at major state changes (goal, VAR, red card). That’s FX‑style maker protection without “last look.”
- Price the rebate against the same P(1−P) geometry takers face, so the net effective vig is flat enough that makers don’t disappear exactly when fans arrive.
